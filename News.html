    PhD thesis proposal defense

    Our doctoral student Dan M. Kazimoto defended a PhD thesis proposal titled “An AI-Agentic Model for Predicting and Explaining Software Release Outcomes in the Era of AI Coding Agents” on 23 July 2026. AI coding agents such as GitHub Copilot, Claude Code, and OpenAI Codex now write code for software projects as teammates. The proposed model forecasts the productivity and quality of a software release, explains each forecast in terms a team can check, and stays valid when AI agents contribute. It also introduces the AI-inclusive bus factor, an ownership measure that counts AI agents alongside people. Asst.   • Jan 2026

    MSR 2026 Mining Challenge paper

    Our paper “When is Generated Code Difficult to Comprehend? Assessing AI Agent Python Code Proficiency in the Wild” was accepted in the Mining Challenge track of MSR 2026, held in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, in April 2026. AI coding agents now write much of the code that developers review, so the team asked how advanced that code is. They mined 591 pull requests with 5,027 Python files written by three AI agents and rated each construct with pycefr, on six levels from A1 (Basic) to C2 (Mastery). Over 90% of the constructs fell into the two basic levels and under 1% reached Mastery, a profile close to that of human pull requests. Nanthit Temkulkiat, the first author, was a 2025 summer intern at The University of Osaka.

    Competition study accepted at MSR 2026

    A paper by Yuki Takei and Prof. Toshiaki Aoki of JAIST and Chaiyong Ragkhitwetsagul of Mahidol University was accepted in the Technical Papers track of MSR 2026, the 23rd International Conference on Mining Software Repositories, which CORE ranks A.   • Dec 2025

    SECURITY.md study accepted in EMSE

    Our paper “Security by Documentation? Characterizing GitHub SECURITY.md Policy and Their Adoption in Python Libraries” has been accepted in Empirical Software Engineering (EMSE), a Q1 journal. Many open-source projects publish a SECURITY.md file that tells users how to report vulnerabilities. Earlier studies counted how many projects adopt such a policy, but they did not examine what the policies say. The study analyzed the security policies of 679 Python libraries from PyPI hosted on GitHub and found that projects with a SECURITY.md file follow recommended security practices more closely. The work grew from internship research by our bachelor's students, with partners at NAIST and The University of Osaka.

  • Oct 2025

    Sprint2Vec in the ASE 2025 Journal-First track

    Our IEEE TSE paper “Sprint2Vec: A Deep Characterization of Sprints in Iterative Software Development” was accepted in the Journal-First track of ASE 2025. Sprint2Vec builds vector representations of Agile sprints from structured data, such as sprint reports, issue attributes, and developer profiles, and from text, such as issue descriptions and sequences of developer activity. Unlike earlier methods that predict only how much a sprint delivers, it predicts both productivity and quality, where quality means the risk of reopened issues. The evaluation covered nearly 8,000 sprints, 118,000 issues, and 3.7 million developer activities across five large open-source projects, where Sprint2Vec beat baseline and state-of-the-art approaches.

  • Sep 2025

    Internship study accepted at ISE 2025

    A study by Chayanid Termphaiboon from a research internship at NAIST was accepted at the International Workshop on Intelligent Software Engineering (ISE 2025), co-located with ASE 2025. It compared Python projects on PyPI with and without a SECURITY.md file to see how security policies relate to dependency management. Projects with a security policy tend to rely on a broader set of direct dependencies, while the depth of their dependency chains is similar. Projects created after SECURITY.md was introduced, especially later adopters, update their dependencies more often. The results suggest that security policies go with more modular projects and more proactive dependency management.

    COVID-19 and remote work study at InCIT 2025

    Our study “The Impact of COVID-19 and Remote Work on Software Development in Thailand” was accepted for publication at InCIT 2025. We surveyed 194 Thai software developers about the challenges and benefits of remote work during the COVID-19 pandemic. The results show no statistically significant differences in productivity or well-being before and after the shift to remote work. As in many global studies, Thai developers met both advantages and challenges, and the survey also found some perspectives specific to Thailand. The findings may also inform other Asian countries and other low- and middle-income economies. We donated to the Siriraj Foundation for every survey response.

    Thai tool adoption study in the ICSME 2025 Journal-First track

    Our EMSE study on how software companies in Thailand adopt automated software engineering tools was accepted in the Journal-First track of ICSME 2025, the International Conference on Software Maintenance and Evolution. The study asked how small and medium-sized software enterprises adopt tools for software measurement, static code analysis, continuous integration, and software testing. Based on a survey of 103 Thai developers, it found that developers are generally familiar with these tools and see their benefits. A lack of knowledge or experience holds back measurement and static analysis tools, continuous integration is used with some difficulty, and manual testing remains common. We call for easier tools that lower the barrier for such companies in developing countries.

    LLM testing paper accepted at NLDB 2025

    Our paper “Test It Before You Trust It: Applying Software Testing for Trustworthy In-context Learning” was accepted at NLDB 2025, the 30th International Conference on Natural Language & Information Systems. The work is a collaboration with the ReaLearn lab of Dr. Teeradaj Racharak, at JAIST and now at Tohoku University. It applies metamorphic testing to large language models on two tasks, sentiment analysis and question answering. It changes the input in ways that keep its meaning, for example by swapping characters, replacing words with synonyms, and switching between active and passive sentences. Tests on OpenAI's GPT-4o and Google's Gemini-2.0-Flash found several cases where the models changed their answers on such inputs.

  • Mar 2024

    Autorepairability at SANER 2024

    Pongpop Lapvikai presented “Autorepairability: A New Software Quality Characteristic” in the ERA track of SANER 2024 in Finland. It comes from Pongpop's 2023 summer internship at the Software Engineering Lab of Osaka University, under the supervision of Prof. Yoshiki Higo. The paper proposes autorepairability as a new characteristic of software quality, meaning the degree to which automated program repair can be applied to a piece of software. Chutweeraya Sriwilailak's APSEC 2024 paper took the idea further by comparing the autorepairability of ChatGPT and Gemini.

  • Mar 2024

    Thai tool adoption study accepted in EMSE

    Our paper “Adoption of Automated Software Engineering Tools and Techniques in Thailand” was accepted in Empirical Software Engineering (EMSE), a Q1 journal. It looks at how aware Thai small and medium-sized software companies are of automated software engineering tools, how far they adopt them, and what challenges they meet, for software measurement, static code analysis, continuous integration, and software testing. We surveyed 103 Thai developers across four groups. The main challenges were a lack of knowledge and experience, long build times in continuous integration, and an emphasis on development over testing. The paper is the second publication of the Newton Fund project “Automated Software Engineering for Thailand Software Industry”, a collaboration between CREST at UCL and SERU since 2018.
